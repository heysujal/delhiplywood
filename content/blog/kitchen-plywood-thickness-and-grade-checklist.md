## Why kitchen plywood decisions go wrong

Most kitchen issues start with two mistakes:

- Wrong grade in moisture-prone areas
- Under-thickness boards for load-bearing cabinets

A modular kitchen looks premium outside, but the long-term life depends on the inside structure.

## Recommended thickness by component

Use this as a practical baseline:

- **Carcass sides/top/bottom:** 18mm
- **Shelves:** 18mm preferred for stability
- **Partitions:** 12mm or 16mm based on design load
- **Back panel:** 6mm or 9mm

If cabinet width is large and heavily loaded, avoid thin shelves.

## Grade selection: where BWR is enough and where BWP is safer

- **General cabinet zones:** good quality BWR often works well
- **Sink unit and high-moisture points:** BWP/Marine is safer
- **Near plumbing lines:** prefer higher moisture resistance

This mixed approach controls cost while protecting critical zones.

## Surface and finishing decisions

Plywood is only one part. Final durability also depends on:

- Laminate thickness and quality
- Edge sealing quality
- Adhesive choice
- Hardware brand and fitting quality

Poor edge finishing can damage even good plywood.

## Pre-installation checklist

Before cutting starts:

1. Match grade and thickness against the approved list.
2. Verify one sample sheet physically.
3. Confirm sink base uses the higher moisture grade.
4. Confirm hardware and board compatibility.
5. Keep buffer quantity for late modifications.

## Post-installation checks

- Open and close shutters multiple times
- Check shelf deflection under load
- Inspect sink base edges and joints
- Confirm all exposed edges are sealed
